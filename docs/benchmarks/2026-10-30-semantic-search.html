Observed ranking and cache behavior

  • In the final five-case set, the two known queries retrieved a gold fact within the top three twice (recall@3 2/2; recall@1 1/2).
  • For the two ambiguous queries, at least one acceptable reference appeared in the top three once (coverage@3 1/2). No threshold was tuned or treated as confidence.
  • The unknown query returned a ranked candidate. There is no abstention rule; the candidate is not asserted as an answer.
  • On the final live run, warm-cache checks had median latency 3.36 ms and p95 4.45 ms, with zero provider dispatches. A free deterministic mock run measured median 1.50 ms and p95 2.56 ms across five warm checks. These are single-run environment timings, not a latency-gain claim.
  • The free checks covered project isolation, same-revision correction invalidation, restart persistence, deletion and stale-revision rejection. They did not use real user memories.
Interpretation: provider pinning plus post-request generation metadata resolved the alias ambiguity in the final run. The eight paid calls and eight synthetic query cases are too small to support a general quality claim. Keep semantic search as opt-in candidate retrieval; keep freshness and scope filtering before provider submission, and do not auto-inject candidates as trusted facts.
